Old Village in Mount Pleasant, SC offers tree-canopied streets, historic cottages, and a genuine Lowcountry waterfront character unlike anywhere else.

A Place Apart: The Character of Old Village

Old Village is the historic heart of Mount Pleasant, South Carolina — a compact, walkable enclave tucked along the Shem Creek and Charleston Harbor waterfront. Long before Mount Pleasant grew into one of the Charleston area's most prominent communities, Old Village was already here: a quiet, self-contained settlement with its own rhythms, its own landmarks, and a deeply rooted sense of place that continues to define it today. Moss-draped oaks arch over narrow streets, and the pace of life slows in a way that feels intentional rather than accidental.

Architecture and Housing Character

The built environment of Old Village reads like a layered history of Lowcountry residential architecture. Modest mid-century cottages sit alongside larger Charleston-style homes and gracious Victorian-era houses, many with deep porches that face the street and catch the coastal breeze. Lot sizes tend to be generous by peninsular standards, and mature landscaping — camellias, azaleas, live oaks — gives the neighborhood a lush, established feel. Because much of Old Village predates modern subdivision patterns, the streetscape has an organic, unhurried quality that newer developments rarely replicate.

Waterfront, Parks, and Green Space

Access to the water is woven into daily life in Old Village. Shem Creek borders the neighborhood to the north, offering a working waterfront lined with docks, paddlers, and the occasional shrimp boat. Waterfront Park, situated along the harbor at the foot of Pitt Street, provides sweeping views of the Charleston skyline across the water and serves as a natural gathering point for the community. The old Pitt Street Bridge — now preserved as a pedestrian walkway extending over the marsh — is one of the neighborhood's most beloved landmarks and a favorite spot for watching sunsets over the creek.

Dining, Shopping, and the Pitt Street Corridor

The Pitt Street corridor functions as Old Village's informal main street. A small collection of long-standing local businesses, a beloved pharmacy with an old-fashioned soda fountain, and a handful of restaurants and cafés give the area a neighborhood-scale commercial character that feels genuinely local rather than curated. This is not a destination retail district — it is the kind of place where residents walk or bike for an errand or a meal and are likely to run into someone they know.

Lifestyle and Local Culture

Life in Old Village is shaped by proximity to the water, the shade of old growth trees, and a strong attachment to the neighborhood's history. Walking and cycling are practical and popular, with the Pitt Street Bridge path and waterfront park forming a natural loop. The community has a longstanding civic identity — one of the oldest in the Mount Pleasant area — and that continuity gives it a distinctive sense of rootedness. Whether arriving by kayak on Shem Creek or on foot along tree-lined Royall Avenue, Old Village rewards those who slow down enough to take it in.
